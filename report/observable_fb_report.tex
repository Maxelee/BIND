\title{\bf Reading the baryon fraction off cluster observables:\\
a field-level proof-of-concept with the BIND emulator}
\author{Max Lee\\[2pt]
\small Columbia University \&\ Center for Computational Astrophysics, Flatiron Institute}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The baryon fraction of a halo, $\fb=M_{\mathrm{b}}/M_{\mathrm{tot}}$, encodes the
integrated history of feedback but is not directly observable: a survey measures
projected thermodynamic fields (thermal Sunyaev--Zel'dovich $\Ysz$, X-ray surface
brightness $\Sx$, gas temperature/entropy/pressure), not masses. We ask whether
these observables are a near-sufficient statistic for $\fb$ at fixed halo mass,
\emph{without} ever inferring the sub-grid feedback parameters $\thetav$. Using the
conditional flow-matching emulator BIND, we paint hydrodynamic fields onto $1111$
fixed dark-matter halos at $256$ Sobol locations in the $30$-dimensional CAMELS
feedback space (cosmology held fixed), forming a clean feedback factorial. We
quantify the residual scatter $\sigma(\fb\mid\mathcal{O})$ on \emph{held-out}
feedback settings, first for integrated scalars and then for stacked radial
profiles. We find that a stacked tSZ$+$X-ray \emph{profile} predicts the
baryon-fraction profile to $69\text{--}80\%$ scatter reduction at fixed mass, a
regime in which the profile shape is decisive (a single integrated $\Ysz$ achieves
only $25\text{--}54\%$). Crucially, applying the map to genuine CAMELS
\emph{hydrodynamic truth} recovers $\fb(r)$ to $\mathrm{RMS}\simeq0.004$, including
the feedback-evacuated core. We show that the super-cosmic differential $\fb(r)$ at
intermediate radii is a real consequence of baryon redistribution rather than a
projection artefact, while the enclosed $\fb(<\Rtwo)$ remains sub-cosmic, and that
the result survives the background-nulled (compensated-aperture) estimator a real
survey must use. All findings are provisional and pertain to one sub-grid model at
fixed cosmology; we delineate the validations required before any astrophysical
claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and objective}
\label{sec:intro}
Baryonic feedback is the dominant astrophysical systematic for weak lensing and a
prime science target for tSZ/X-ray surveys, yet the quantity that most directly
summarises it---the halo baryon fraction $\fb$---is not an observable. Observers
measure line-of-sight integrals of the gas thermodynamics; the partition of mass
into dark matter, gas, and stars is inferred only through modelling. The sub-grid
feedback parameters $\thetav$ of any one simulation suite are nuisance labels of a
particular model, not measurable quantities.

This motivates a sharper, falsifiable question. \emph{At fixed halo mass, do the
observables $\mathcal{O}=\{\Ysz,\Sx,\dots\}$ determine $\fb$, on feedback settings
never seen in training---i.e.\ in a parameter-free regime?} If so, a field-level
emulator trained across feedback space becomes a tool to read an unmeasurable
physical state off real data. The scientific deliverable is therefore not a point
prediction but the \emph{conditional scatter} $\sigma(\fb\mid\mathcal{O})$ and how it
shrinks as observables are added: a tight mean relation with large scatter is not a
usable inverse map. The test \emph{fails} (a publishable null) if
$\sigma(\fb\mid\Ysz)\approx\sigma(\fb)$ within a mass bin, and \emph{succeeds} if
observables substantially reduce the scatter.

\section{Data: emulator and feedback design}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{The BIND emulator}
BIND is a conditional flow-matching generative model that paints baryonic fields
onto a dark-matter-only (DMO) projection for the CAMELS IllustrisTNG suite. For a
halo with DMO condition $c$ and parameter vector $\thetav\in\mathbb{R}^{35}$
(cosmological $+$ astrophysical), it draws the hydrodynamic state
$x_1=[\,\mathrm{DM},\mathrm{Gas},\mathrm{Stars},y,T,K,P_e\,]$ as $128\times128$ maps.
Training uses optimal-transport flow matching: with $x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{1})$
and interpolant $x_t=(1-t)\,x_0+t\,x_1$, a UNet $v_\phi(x_t,t\mid c,\thetav)$
regresses the velocity $\dot{x}_t=x_1-x_0$, and sampling integrates
$\dot{x}=v_\phi$ from $t{=}0$ to $1$ (here $20$ Euler steps). Parameters enter through
an encoder summed with the diffusion-time embedding to drive adaptive group
normalisation; fields are standardised channel-wise as $\log_{10}(1+x)$ (mass) or
$\log_{10}x$ (thermo). We use the released \texttt{fm\_thermo} exponential-moving-%
average weights.

\subsection{The Sobol feedback factorial}
\label{sec:sobol}
We hold cosmology fixed at the CAMELS fiducial
($\Omega_m=0.3$, $\sigma_8=0.8$, $\Omega_b=0.049$, $h=0.671$, $n_s=0.962$, so the
cosmic baryon fraction $f_{\mathrm{cos}}\equiv\Omega_b/\Omega_m=0.163$) and vary the
$30$ astrophysical parameters. A scrambled Sobol sequence
$\{\bm{u}_d\}_{d=1}^{256}\subset[0,1]^{30}$ (a low-discrepancy quasi-random set;
$256=2^8$ preserves its balance) is mapped into physical parameters through the
emulator's normalised box,
\begin{equation}
\theta^{(j)}_{d}=
\begin{cases}
\theta^{j}_{\min}+u^{j}_{d}\,(\theta^{j}_{\max}-\theta^{j}_{\min}), & \text{linear } j,\\[2pt]
10^{\,\theta^{j}_{\min}+u^{j}_{d}\,(\theta^{j}_{\max}-\theta^{j}_{\min})}, & \text{log-flagged } j,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
so log-flagged parameters are sampled log-uniformly within the training domain. For
each design $d$ we paint the \emph{same} $1111$ CV halos (drawn from $26$ CV
realisations, $\log_{10}\Mtwo\in[13.0,14.8]$), resetting the sampling noise seed
before every design so that per-halo initial noise is shared across $d$
(common random numbers). Differences across $d$ are thus the pure response to
feedback. The result is a true factorial: halo identity $\times$ feedback. The design
matrix $\thetav$ is used \emph{only} as the cross-validation grouping label
(\S\ref{sec:methods})---never as a model input.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Apertures, observables, and field-level stacks}
\label{sec:stacks}
Each $128\times128$ patch spans a $6.25\,\hMpc$ cutout, giving a pixel scale
$\Delta=48.83\,\hkpc$ with the halo at the centre. For per-halo scalars we use a
circular $\Rtwo$ aperture $\mathcal{A}_i=\{|\bm{x}-\bm{x}_c|\le \Rtwo^{(i)}\}$ and
define, with pixel area $A_{\rm pix}=\Delta^2$,
\begin{align}
\Ysz^{(i)} &= A_{\rm pix}\!\!\sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_i}\! y_p, &
\fb^{(i)} &= \frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_i}(\mathrm{Gas}+\mathrm{Stars})_p}
{\sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_i}(\mathrm{DM}+\mathrm{Gas}+\mathrm{Stars})_p}, \\
\Sx^{(i)} &= \sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_i}\!\mathrm{Gas}_p^{2}\,\sqrt{T_p}, &
\{T,K,P_e\}^{(i)} &= \frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_i}\{T,K,P_e\}_p\,\mathrm{Gas}_p}
{\sum_{p\in\mathcal{A}_i}\mathrm{Gas}_p}.
\end{align}
$\Sx\propto n_e^2\sqrt{T}$ is a bremsstrahlung emission-measure proxy (no
instrument response or cooling-function normalisation); thermodynamics are
gas-mass weighted to suppress empty pixels.

For the profile analysis we stack at the field level. Within mass bin $m$ and design
$d$ the in-bin halos are co-registered (all centred) and averaged per channel; the
nonlinear $\Sx$ is formed per halo then averaged (one stacks images, not derived
scalars). Azimuthal averaging into $N_r{=}12$ pixel-defined annuli $\{r_k\}$
(geometric centres $57\text{--}1595\,\hkpc$; pixel-defined to avoid empty sub-pixel
bins) gives the stacked profiles
\begin{equation}
\Ysz(r_k)=\big\langle y_i\big\rangle_{i\in m},\quad
\Sx(r_k)=\big\langle \mathrm{Gas}_i^2\sqrt{T_i}\big\rangle_{i\in m},\quad
\fb(r_k)=\frac{\big\langle \mathrm{Gas}_i{+}\mathrm{Stars}_i\big\rangle_{i\in m}}
{\big\langle \mathrm{DM}_i{+}\mathrm{Gas}_i{+}\mathrm{Stars}_i\big\rangle_{i\in m}} ,
\end{equation}
each azimuthally averaged at radius $r_k$ over mass bin $m$ and design $d$.
$\fb(r)$ is thus a \emph{projected, differential} (annular) baryon fraction; we
contrast it with the enclosed $\fb(<r)$ in \S\ref{sec:proj}. Bin populations are
$\{828,232,51\}$ halos. Figure~\ref{fig:profiles} shows the resulting stacked
profiles and their feedback-driven scatter across the $256$ designs---the signal the
map must explain.

\subsection{The predictive map and the conditional scatter}
\label{sec:map}
The deliverable is the conditional scatter of $\fb$ given a feature set
$\mathcal{O}$, estimated out-of-sample on held-out feedback. Let $\hat g$ be a
regressor trained on a subset of designs. On held-out designs we measure
\begin{equation}
\sigma(\fb\mid\mathcal{O}) = \sqrt{\E\big[(\fb-\hat g(\mathcal{O}))^2\big]},\qquad
\rho_{\rm red}=1-\frac{\sigma(\fb\mid\mathcal{O})}{\sigma_{\rm marg}(\fb)},
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{\rm marg}$ the in-bin standard deviation (the no-information baseline)
and $\rho_{\rm red}\in(-\infty,1]$ the fractional reduction. The held-out split is by
\emph{design}: \texttt{GroupKFold}$(\thetav)$ for per-halo scalars,
\texttt{KFold} over the $256$ designs for profiles (each design is one stacked
sample). This is the parameter-free test---the map must generalise to feedback it
never saw. The resampling/independent unit is always the design, not the halo
(halos are not independent across designs); uncertainties use a design-level
bootstrap. For scalars (per-halo, $\sim10^5$ rows/bin) we use gradient-boosted trees;
for profiles ($256$ design-level samples, highly collinear) we use multi-output ridge
regression,
\begin{equation}
\hat{\bm{\beta}}=\arg\min_{\bm{\beta}}\;\|\bm{F}-\bm{X}\bm{\beta}\|_F^2+\alpha\|\bm{\beta}\|_F^2,
\qquad \alpha=10\ \text{(fixed, to avoid tuning leakage)},
\end{equation}
with $\bm{F}$ the matrix of $\fb(r_k)$ targets, $\bm{X}$ the standardised
(log-)profile features, and per-radius reduction
$\rho_{\rm red}(r_k)=1-\sigma(r_k)/\sigma_{\rm marg}(r_k)$. As an information
summary we report $R^2_{\rm info}=1-e^{-2I(\fb;\mathcal{O}\mid m)}$ via
$I=-\tfrac12\ln(1-\rho^2_{R})$ from the held-out skill. Conclusions are insensitive
to $\alpha\in[1,100]$.

\paragraph{Compensated aperture photometry.}
A real stacked measurement cannot access the raw profile: the line-of-sight (LOS)
background is unknown. The standard estimator is compensated aperture photometry
(CAP),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CAP}_\mathcal{O}(\theta)=\big\langle \mathcal{O}\big\rangle_{r<\theta}
-\big\langle \mathcal{O}\big\rangle_{\theta<r<\sqrt{2}\,\theta},
\label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
which annihilates any spatially uniform background exactly. Because (\ref{eq:cap}) is
a linear, radius-only functional, we evaluate it exactly from the stored azimuthal
profiles and per-annulus pixel counts---no re-reduction---and use it in
\S\ref{sec:cap}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Integrated scalars: $\Ysz$ alone is degenerate; $\Sx$ breaks it}
\label{sec:scalar}
Table~\ref{tab:scalar} gives the held-out-$\thetav$ scatter reduction for per-halo
integrated quantities. Within a mass bin, $\Ysz$ alone is a \emph{weak} predictor of
$\fb$ ($9\text{--}26\%$): tSZ measures pressure $\propto n_eT$ and trades gas content
against temperature, so many $(\fb,T)$ pairs share a $\Ysz$. Adding the X-ray $\Sx$
($\propto n_e^2\sqrt{T}$), which weights density differently, roughly doubles the
reduction; the full thermodynamic vector reaches $49\text{--}64\%$. The map is
therefore well-posed as a \emph{vector}, not from $\Ysz$ alone. (The kSZ optical-depth
proxy $\tau\propto$ gas mass is near-degenerate with the $\fb$ numerator and is
excluded as circular.)

\subsection{The observable--$\fb$ joint distribution and profile prediction}
\label{sec:profile}
The $256$ designs at fixed $(r,m)$ are a sample of the joint $p(\fb(r),\Ysz(r))$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:joint}). The regressor learns its conditional mean
$\E[\fb\mid\Ysz]$; the residual scatter we report is the conditional width; and
because the points are indexed by feedback, the locus is a feedback sequence.
Moving from integrated scalars to \emph{profiles} produces a qualitative change.
At the population/profile level $\Ysz$ alone becomes a \emph{strong} predictor
(mean-over-radius reduction $\{69,73,67\}\%$ across mass bins), because stacking
removes per-halo scatter and the profile \emph{shape} disambiguates feedback states
that a single integrated $\Ysz$ conflates. Adding $\Sx$ and the thermodynamics
raises this to $\{73,77,70\}\%$ and $\{80,80,75\}\%$ respectively
(Fig.~\ref{fig:skill}). The reduction peaks ($\sim90\%$) at $r\simeq100\text{--}200
\,\hkpc$---the feedback-active region---and falls in the outskirts where
$\fb\!\to\!f_{\mathrm{cos}}$ and the design-to-design variation collapses. That shape
carries the information is shown directly by predicting the design-mean $\fb$: a
single integrated $\Ysz$ yields $\{54,49,25\}\%$, whereas the $\Ysz(r)$ \emph{profile}
yields $\{79,73,60\}\%$.

\subsection{Validation against hydrodynamic truth}
\label{sec:truth}
The decisive test maps observables to $\fb$ on genuine hydrodynamic fields. We train
the map on the $256$ BIND designs and apply it to the $27$-realisation CV set at
\emph{fiducial} feedback, stacking the CAMELS \emph{hydro-truth} mass and thermo
fields with the identical geometry (the truth mass patches are cut from the full box
with a convention validated to reproduce the stored DMO condition to correlation
unity). The truth observables fall entirely within the BIND design cloud, so this is
an interpolation. Figure~\ref{fig:realdata} shows the outcome: feeding the
\emph{truth} $\{\Ysz,\Sx\}$ profiles recovers the \emph{truth} $\fb(r)$ to
$\mathrm{RMS}\simeq0.004$ (Table~\ref{tab:truth}), $\sim4\%$ of the $\fb$ range and
$\sim\!\tfrac15\sigma_{\rm marg}$, including the feedback-evacuated core
($\fb\!\simeq\!0.08$ at $\sim100\,\hkpc$) that differs from the BIND design mean---so
the map genuinely uses the observations. Two honest features stand out. First,
$\Ysz$ alone again underperforms $\Ysz{+}\Sx$ on truth. Second, the \emph{full}
thermodynamic vector, best \emph{in-distribution}, transfers \emph{worse} (e.g.\
$16\times10^{-3}$ in the sparse top bin): the richer feature set overfits the
emulator$\to$truth domain gap. We therefore identify $\{\Ysz,\Sx\}$ as the robust
real-data feature set.

\subsection{Projection: the super-cosmic $\fb(r)$ is physical, not an artefact}
\label{sec:proj}
The differential $\fb(r)$ exceeds $f_{\mathrm{cos}}$ at $r\gtrsim400\,\hkpc$, which is
impossible for an \emph{enclosed} baryon fraction and prompts a projection check. We
re-stack the same halos from the $6.25\,\hMpc$ cluster-depth cube products
(an $8\times$ shorter LOS). Figure~\ref{fig:proj} shows that (i) the $6.25$ and
$50\,\hMpc$ differential profiles are nearly identical (LOS effect $<0.02$, only at
$r\gtrsim600\,\hkpc$, and the longer LOS pulls the profile \emph{towards}
$f_{\mathrm{cos}}$, never inflating it); (ii) the super-cosmic differential is
\emph{real}---feedback evacuates the core and deposits gas at intermediate radii, so
the annular $\fb$ locally overshoots; and (iii) the \emph{enclosed}
$\fb(<\Rtwo)=\{0.11,0.15,0.16\}$ stays below $f_{\mathrm{cos}}=0.163$, the genuine
``missing baryon'' signal, with the deepest deficit at low mass. Differential and
enclosed fractions were simply being conflated; no baryon-conservation problem
exists.

\subsection{The result survives the background-nulled (CAP) estimator}
\label{sec:cap}
The mass-based $\fb$ target is LOS-robust, but $\Ysz\propto\!\int\!P\,d\ell$ is not:
it integrates diffuse, extended pressure plus the two-halo and uncorrelated terms (a
real stacked $\Ysz$ flattens to a uniform background at large $r$; $\Sx\propto n_e^2$
is far more concentrated). We therefore re-run the truth validation with CAP-filtered
observables (\ref{eq:cap}), the estimator a survey is forced to use. The map still
recovers the truth $\fb(r)$, to $\mathrm{RMS}\simeq0.005\text{--}0.007$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cap}, left), modestly worse than the raw profile because CAP discards
the absolute zero-point---information the raw run had but a measurement could not. The
meaningful statement is that the result no longer depends on an unmeasurable $\Ysz$
normalisation, which strengthens its real-data relevance. Contrary to our initial
expectation, CAP is \emph{not} more robust to per-bin measurement noise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cap}, right): the per-measurement error grows roughly linearly for
both raw and CAP and approaches $\sigma_{\rm marg}$ by $\sim20\%$ noise. CAP buys
robustness against the \emph{background}, not against per-bin noise; the LOS-robust,
whole-profile fit already averages the latter.

\section{Discussion and threats to validity}
\label{sec:disc}
The proof-of-concept succeeds within its scope: a stacked tSZ$+$X-ray profile
predicts the baryon-fraction profile of a halo population to $\sim70\text{--}80\%$
scatter reduction on unseen feedback, recovers genuine CAMELS hydrodynamic $\fb(r)$
to $\mathrm{RMS}\simeq0.004$, and survives the background-nulled estimator. The
information lives in the profile shape and in the core/feedback-active region; a
single tSZ amplitude is insufficient. We stress the boundaries.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{One sub-grid model, fixed cosmology.} All results are CAMELS-TNG at a
single cosmology. ``Parameter-free'' is demonstrated only within one feedback family;
off-grid generalisation (SIMBA/Astrid, and ultimately real data) is the eventual
point and is untested here. Marginalising cosmology will add scatter and may reopen
degeneracies, as $\Ysz$ and $\fb$ both respond to $\Omega_m$.
\item \textbf{Interpolation, not extrapolation.} The CV truth is a single
(fiducial) feedback point lying inside the design cloud. Behaviour at the feedback
extremes is not validated.
\item \textbf{Shared halos.} The truth halos share DMO structure with training; the
truth observables and $\fb$ are independent of any fit, and stacking is at the
population level, but a held-out-halo test remains desirable.
\item \textbf{Observable realism.} $\Sx$ is an uncalibrated $n_e^2\sqrt{T}$ proxy;
$\fb(r)$ is a $2$D projected ratio, not a $3$D enclosed mass fraction; the injected
noise is a uniform-in-radius toy. The CAP background is computed from the
$50\,\hMpc$ projection's profile---a fully realistic $\Ysz$ LOS treatment requires
$6.25\,\hMpc$ \emph{thermo} cubes, which the current cube products lack.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Next steps.} In priority order: (i) generate $6.25\,\hMpc$ thermo cubes to
quantify the $\Ysz$ LOS background that CAP removes and re-validate with CAP-filtered
inputs end to end; (ii) an off-grid test on a distinct sub-grid model; (iii) a
held-out-halo split; (iv) propagation to a forward weak-lensing/$\fb$ systematic.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conc}
Treating the problem as the conditional density $p(\fb\mid\mathcal{O})$ rather than a
mean relation, we have shown---within CAMELS-TNG at fixed cosmology---that measurable
tSZ$+$X-ray \emph{profiles} determine the unmeasurable baryon-fraction profile of a
halo population at fixed mass, on feedback settings never seen in training, and that
the map transfers to genuine hydrodynamic truth and to the background-nulled
estimator a real survey must use. The deliverable is the scatter and its reduction,
and it is substantial ($\sim70\text{--}80\%$). The remaining work is generalisation:
LOS-correct thermo cubes, off-grid sub-grid models, and cosmology marginalisation,
before any astrophysical claim.
